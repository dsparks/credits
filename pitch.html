The problem

Right now, losing games is a team-building strategy.

The intent of the current lottery is to allocate young talent to teams that need it, but teams can just manipulate their losses to give the appearance of need. We need a system that provides help to teams that “need” it, but without referring to losses.

The solution

Team-Building Credits

Credits, not losses, determine draft position. Two straightforward changes:

  • Rather than base lottery odds on losing and complicated protections, teams bid Credits for draft position, with a fair lottery system that still provides some randomness.
  • The league allocates 100 Credits to each team, each year. Teams that advance in the playoffs get 20 fewer for each series they win.

That’s it - everything else stays the same. No complicated central planning rules. The market determines what everything costs, and teams determine when they want to “focus” on the draft. And the system is super-flexible, to assist with any league priorities or adjust if there are issues.

Talent allocation

Rebuilding teams still get help

The NBA has always prioritized giving non-contending teams a path to build through the draft. Credits preserve that, without encouraging anyone to lose intentionally.

Playoff offset

Teams that win playoff series receive fewer Credits. Most teams get 100, but for each series you win, you get 20 fewer. So the champion only gets 20.

Save up

Credits roll over. A team can bank Credits for 2-3 years, then bid aggressively when the right draft class comes along.

Trade for more

Rebuilding teams can trade veteran players for other teams’ Credits to further accumulate draft capital, just like trading for picks today.

Lottery

Rolling Window Lottery

A new lottery method lets teams bid Credits to determine how good their pick(s) are, with some uncertainty.
  1. In late April, each team secretly submits a Credit bid for each pick they have.
  2. In early May, the league publishes all the bids.
  3. On lottery night, for the first pick, the top 4 highest bidders are put into a lottery amongst themselves, with probability proportional to the number of Credits each bid toward that slot.
  4. The winner of this mini-lottery gets the first pick, gets charged the bid of the lowest bidder among those four teams, and is removed from the pool.
  5. For the second pick, the #5 bidder is added to the three non-winners of the first pick, and another lottery is held.
  6. The winner of this lottery gets the second pick, and the #6 bidder is added into the pool for the third pick, and so on, until all picks have been assigned.

To see how it works in action, see an example here or try the simulator.

Lottery

Rolling Window Lottery

Some other lottery rules:
  • Teams can have more than one “pick slot” in either round of the draft (since you can still trade for other teams’ pick slots). Each slot is associated with a separate bid.
  • Teams may bid 0; they pick at the end of the first round, with a random tiebreaker. (All ties are broken randomly.)
  • Bidding for the second round happens after the Rolling Window Lottery; picks are allocated in order of bid size, with ties broken randomly.
  • The second apron draft pick penalty is easily accommodated; a frozen pick slot can’t be traded; and a frozen pick that goes to the end of the first round simply can’t have a bid >0. (For debate: whether that team still gets any Credits that year.)

Trade market

The trade market gets better, more liquid

Credits are a new tradeable asset: more flexible than picks, more meaningful than cash.

For example

A contending team trades a developing young player, a 2034 first-round slot, and 35 Credits to a rebuilding team, in exchange for a playoff-caliber starter.

  • A single Credit can replace the classic heavily-protected second-round pick as minimum trade consideration.
  • Future “pick slots” and Credit allocations can be traded (all-or-nothing for a given year)
  • Pick protections and swap options become irrelevant; the bidding process handles all of that.

League tools

A flexible tool for the league

Enforcement

Where the league currently fines teams money — for resting players, bad injury reports, tampering — it could dock Credits instead. Rich owners can absorb a fine. Losing future Credits is a basketball penalty, not a financial one. And it’s far more proportionate than stripping draft picks.

Incentives

The league could reward competition by awarding bonus Credits to In-Season Tournament finalists, or to the teams of players on the winning All-Star roster. The system is flexible enough to support virtually any league priority.

Transition

The Transition

Due to outstanding traded picks, this system likely can’t be implemented for at least 4-6 years. The fairest transition will depend on draft rules enacted in the interim.

  • Prior to implementation we’d expect lots of study and playtesting of the system
  • Once the system is agreed to, teams should be able to trade future pick slots and Credit allocations immediately.
  • We could imagine a transition where teams begin receiving smaller amounts of Credits 3-4 years prior to full implementation, so that the weakest teams entering the system have an advantage. See link at end of presentation for more details of this proposal, but other transitions are possible too.

Fans deserve a league where their team never has a reason to lose.

This proposal is a starting point (and requires further study/playtesting/etc), but this plan leaves room for modification. The specific numbers are all tunable, and because it relies on market forces, nearly all the concepts can be altered before or during system implementation. The core principle is simple: sever the link between losing and draft position, and let the market do the rest.
